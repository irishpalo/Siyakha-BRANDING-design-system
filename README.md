# Siyakha Branding design system

Siyakha Branding is a brand system built around the staggered brick mark, Zilla Slab set against Work Sans, and a Charcoal-on-Ivory palette accented in Terracotta. Charcoal #2B211B carries the logo, headings and key brand moments; Ivory #FAF6F0 is the primary ground; Terracotta #C15F3C is reserved for calls to action, links and the BRANDING descriptor. Zilla Slab is used for headlines and brand-led display text, Work Sans for body copy, interface text, labels and utility content. Photography is the system's primary storytelling device: real people, real production, documentary rather than advertising. Illustration and iconography are functional rather than decorative, built on the structural logic of the brick logomark, outline only, with no fills, gradients or shadows. Data visualisation follows the same restraint: flat, minimally decorated, and coloured from the established brand hierarchy rather than a separate chart palette.

## How to use this

Master logo artwork is supplied as SVG for digital and scalable applications, with PDF/EPS versions for professional print and production. Always reproduce the logo from this approved master artwork; never redraw, distort, rotate, stretch, add effects to, or alter its proportions. The logotype should not be recreated by typing SIYAKHA in Zilla Slab: use the approved artwork so proportions, spacing and alignment are preserved.

The relationship between the brick mark, the Siyakha wordmark and the Branding descriptor in the combination mark is fixed. Do not independently resize, reposition or rearrange these elements.

Maintain clear space around the logo, derived proportionally from a consistent unit within the brick mark, so that no text, imagery, borders or other graphic elements enter it.

Test the combination mark at small display sizes before deployment. If Branding becomes illegible, use the logomark or a simplified configuration rather than reducing the complete lockup further. Test the logomark independently for favicon use, social avatars, small UI placements, product marking, embroidery and engraving.

The primary logo colour is Charcoal #2B211B on an Ivory #FAF6F0 ground; a reversed version may be used where required for contrast. Terracotta is reserved for the BRANDING descriptor and should not compete with the primary Charcoal wordmark or logomark. Do not recolour the logo using the wider supporting palette unless a specific production requirement calls for single-colour reproduction.

Take colour, type and icon assignments from the values fixed in this guide rather than approximating any of them.

## Direction

The combination mark, the brick mark to the left of the wordmark with Branding beneath, is the preferred primary logo and the default full-brand identity. The logomark and logotype are supporting configurations rather than separate identities: the logomark is used when space is limited or the brand is already recognisable, the logotype when the name needs to be the primary identifier.

Across photography, illustration, iconography and data visualisation, the governing principle is restraint: emphasise information through contrast and hierarchy rather than decoration or bright colour. Photography should feel observational rather than staged. Illustration should feel like an extension of the logo rather than a separate visual language. Data visualisation should communicate through structure, scale and contrast rather than embellishment. The established colour hierarchy carries through all of them: Charcoal for primary emphasis, Warm Brown for secondary emphasis, Stone for structural context, Terracotta for action and interaction, Forest Green for community and impact.

## Spacing

A single spacing scale is used across the system: 8px, 16px, 24px, 32px and 48px. Apply it consistently between text elements, buttons and calls to action, card contents, sections and layout blocks, so vertical and horizontal rhythm stays uniform across every surface. Data visualisation follows its own fixed spacing values, set out under Data Visualisation below, rather than this general scale.

## Components

Structural UI components carry the same colour and type hierarchy as the rest of the system rather than introducing their own.

**Button:** Primary and Secondary variants, each available as Filled or Outline. Primary Filled uses Terracotta as the fill, matching Terracotta's role as the system's primary action colour. Secondary Outline uses a Charcoal outline on an Ivory or transparent ground. Button text follows the Label style: Work Sans Semibold, 14px.

**Navbar:** Light and Dark variants. Light uses an Ivory ground with Charcoal type and the standard combination mark; Dark uses a Charcoal ground with Ivory type and the reversed combination mark. The logo sits left-aligned in both.

**Footer:** Light and Dark variants, following the same ground and logo-colourway pairing as the Navbar.

**Card:** built from a photograph, an H-style header (Zilla Slab), P: Regular body copy (Work Sans) and a Terracotta text link. Stone is the card surface, consistent with Stone's role for cards and content sections under Colour above.

## Photography

Photography is Siyakha BRANDING's primary storytelling device. It should make the work feel tangible, skilled and human, observational rather than staged: the objective is to document the work, people and production process rather than manufacture a polished advertising scene.

**Style:** real people and real production; documentary rather than advertising; natural light where possible; matte, low-gloss finish; warm, grounded colour treatment; preserve the natural character and texture of materials.

**Subjects to prioritise:** hands mid-stitch; thread on spools; garments in production; presses during debossing or printing; UV and engraving equipment in use; materials before production; finished branded products being inspected, packed or delivered; makers working on or handling finished pieces.

**People:** feature the actual makers whenever possible; show collaboration between clients and the production team; include diverse South African representation; show people actively working rather than simply posing; avoid staged corporate imagery and generic handshake photographs.

**Composition:** close-ups for craft, material and production detail; medium shots for individual production processes; wide shots for workshop environments and context; use negative space deliberately for copy placement; keep the subject clearly identifiable within the frame.

**Technical standards:** minimum resolution 3000px on the longest edge; shoot in RAW where possible; maintain consistent white balance across a photographic set; preserve texture in fabric, leather, paper, metal and other production materials; avoid processing that removes natural material detail.

**Avoid:** generic stock photography; overly polished studio scenes; excessive depth-of-field effects; dramatic cinematic effects that obscure the subject; artificial HDR processing; heavy filters; smiling-at-camera product poses.

Every photograph should answer at least one of these questions: who made this, how was this made, what material is being worked, why does this quality hold up, what does the finished brand feel like in someone's hands.

## Illustration

Illustration is a functional communication tool. It should explain information, clarify processes and make technical concepts easier to understand, and should never be used simply to fill empty space.

**Role:** explain branding techniques, show production steps, compare production options, clarify specifications and support educational content.

**Style:** simple and functional; geometric rather than expressive; based on the structural logic of Siyakha BRANDING's brick logo; minimal detail; consistent proportions; no decorative textures, shadows, gradients or photorealistic treatment. The system should feel like an extension of the logo rather than a separate visual language.

**Visual rules:** a maximum of two colours within a single illustration. Charcoal #2B211B for lines, structures and primary information. Terracotta #C15F3C selectively, to identify the key action, feature or point of emphasis. Do not introduce additional colours simply to make an illustration more visually interesting. Maintain a consistent stroke weight; rounded corners may be used where they improve clarity or legibility, but should not become a decorative characteristic.

**Layout:** pair illustrations with short explanatory labels; maintain consistent spacing between elements; give each illustration sufficient surrounding space; present one concept at a time; break complex processes into sequential illustrations rather than combining everything into one graphic.

**Use cases:** embroidery versus DTC comparisons; debossing depth examples; print durability comparisons; artwork setup guides; production timelines; packaging diagrams; sizing and specification diagrams.

**Avoid:** decorative mascots; hand-drawn doodles; 3D illustrations; complex isometric scenes; decorative textures; excessive detail; illustration used only to fill space.

## Iconography

The icon system should support clarity, not decoration. Icons identify branding techniques, production stages and functional actions at a glance.

**Construction:** built on a consistent 24px grid with a 1.5px stroke; the same construction logic and optical weight throughout. Outline icons only, no fills, gradients, shadows or decorative effects; consistent stroke weight, corner treatment and proportions.

**Technique icons:** one icon per primary branding technique, kept consistent throughout the brand system. Where an icon becomes ambiguous at small sizes, legibility takes precedence over literal representation.

| Technique | Icon |
| --- | --- |
| Embroidery | Check circle |
| Screen print | Shopping bag |
| DTC | Auto awesome |
| UV | QR code |
| Engraving | Diamond |
| Pad printing | Rocket launch |
| Debossing | Verified / card membership |

**Colour:** Charcoal #2B211B as the default icon colour. Terracotta should not be used to create decorative variation between icons; if an icon requires emphasis within an interface, follow the established interaction-colour rules.

**Size:** 16px minimum size; 24px standard UI size; 32px feature size. Icons must remain recognisable and visually balanced at all three.

**Use:** technique pages, product cards, quote forms, packaging references, educational comparisons, production process guides.

**Avoid:** mixed filled and outline icons; uneven stroke weights; mixed icon families; stock icon packs; decorative circular badges; icons used without a clear functional or informational purpose.

## Data Visualisation

Data visualisation should make decisions easier, not make reports look more decorative. The purpose is to communicate performance, comparison, trends and priority with minimal cognitive effort.

**Purpose:** show what matters first, support business decisions, explain trends and comparisons, make trade-offs visible and reduce the amount of reading required.

**Style:** minimal decoration; flat design only; generous white space; consistent chart dimensions and spacing; clear hierarchy; no gradients, shadows or 3D effects. Charts should communicate through structure, scale and contrast rather than visual embellishment.

**Colour rules:** Charcoal #2B211B as the primary data colour and for the most important value or category. Warm Brown #6B4A3A for secondary data. Stone #EDE1D3 for background tracks, subtle grids and contextual areas. Terracotta #C15F3C reserved primarily for CTAs, links, active states and other interface actions rather than general-purpose data. Forest Green #1F4A3A only for data relating to community, jobs, skills, opportunity or impact outcomes. Use one primary emphasis colour per chart; do not introduce multiple bright colours simply to differentiate categories. Sand #D8C7B3, Clay #B8957A and Ash #8A7A6D retain their established roles elsewhere in the brand system rather than becoming default chart colours.

**Chart selection:** use the simplest chart type that communicates the information accurately. Bar charts for category comparisons; line charts for trends over time; stacked bars for composition; tables when exact values matter more than visual comparison. Pie charts should generally be avoided, used only when there are five categories or fewer and showing proportional composition is genuinely useful.

**Ordering:** largest to smallest for ranked comparisons; chronological order for time-based data; natural process order for workflows; alphabetical order where no meaningful ranking exists. Maintain the same ordering across related charts.

**Labelling:** label axes clearly, in sentence case, with units such as R, %, units or days included; avoid unnecessary abbreviations; use consistent terminology; keep labels close enough to the relevant data to avoid ambiguity.

**Numbers:** round numbers when exact precision is not necessary; use exact values in tables when required; maintain consistent decimal places within the same chart; do not imply precision the underlying data does not support.

**Annotation:** use annotation only when it adds interpretation. Highlight the single most important insight rather than annotating every data point, for example "Embroidery retained colour after 50 washes." Callouts should be short and directly connected to the relevant data.

**Spacing:** 24px between charts; 16px between a chart and its caption; 8px between a chart title and the chart itself. Maintain consistent spacing throughout reports and presentations.

**Avoid:** rainbow colour palettes; heavy gridlines; decorative icons inside charts; unnecessary dual axes; 3D bars; donut charts; gauges; decorative patterns; excessive annotation; colour used without a defined informational purpose.

**Brand principle:** emphasise information through contrast and hierarchy, not through bright colour. The system follows Siyakha BRANDING's established colour hierarchy: Charcoal for primary emphasis, Warm Brown for secondary emphasis, Stone for structural context, Terracotta for action and interaction, Forest Green for community and impact. For a technique comparison, use a ranked horizontal bar chart with Charcoal for the recommended technique, Warm Brown for alternatives and Stone for contextual tracks or backgrounds. The governing principle is simple: one clear message, one clear emphasis and no unnecessary decoration.
